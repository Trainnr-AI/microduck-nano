# The SCS0009: what its maker says and what one servo did

What is known about the robot's servo on 2026-11-24, from Feetech's specification and from one servo on the bench.
Nothing here is a fitted model: that needs a known load on the horn and a fix in trainnr
([sim-to-real.md](../design/sim-to-real.md), stage 3).

Waveshare sells the same servo as "SC09" and its sheet agrees where it overlaps (2.3 kg cm and 0.1 s / 60 degrees
at 6 V, 1.0 A locked, 150 mA no load), except for the input voltage, which it gives as 4.8 to 8.4 V
([PDF](https://download.mikroe.com/documents/datasheets/SC09_Servo_datasheet.pdf)). Feetech's 7.4 V is the one to
keep to.

The registers the bench read at addresses 21 to 27 are, by Waveshare's
[memory table for SC servos](https://docs.waveshare.com/Memory_Map_Explanation/SC_Servo_Memory_Map_Explanation):
the position loop's P coefficient at 21 (ours reads 15), the smallest starting force at 24 as two bytes in tenths
of a percent (ours reads 45: 4.5 %), and the dead zone each way at 26 and 27 in steps (ours read 1 and 1).

## What follows from those numbers

Arithmetic on the table above, not measurements.

- **Per volt:** stall torque 0.038 N m, no-load speed 1.75 rad/s (100 deg/s). Both lines pass close to zero, so
  either scales with the supply: at 5.0 V, 0.19 N m and 500 deg/s; at 5.8 V, 0.22 N m and 580 deg/s.
- **Torque lost per unit of speed** (what a motor model calls back-EMF, with the gearbox's viscous friction in
  it): stall torque over no-load speed, 0.0216 N m s/rad.
- **The gearbox wastes more than half.** Electrically the motor's constant is 6 V / 10.5 rad/s = 0.57 V s/rad at
  the output; mechanically it delivers 2.3 kg cm per amp = 0.23 N m/A. For a lossless drive the two are equal;
  here the ratio is 0.39. A servo like this is hard to turn by hand and gives little back when the load drives it.
- **Rated is a third of stall.** 0.75 kg cm of 2.3. The design review found that standing on one leg asks 74 to
  77 % of stall of the stance hip roll ([review](design-review-2026-11-14.md)): well over twice the rated torque,
  close to the 80 % at which the servo's own overload protection starts counting, and at about 0.75 A a servo.
  That is the number to watch when a gait exists.
- **Current for the robot.** Fifteen servos stalled at once would draw 15 A at 6 V; fifteen at rated load, 6 A.

## What the simulation model already assumes

The design's model (`robot_scs0009_v2.xml`, the actuator class every joint uses) is these datasheet numbers at
5 V, which is why trainnr calls its actuators "declared, not identified":

| In the model | Value | The datasheet's |
|---|---|---|
| Torque limit (`forcerange`) | 0.1883 N m | stall at 5.0 V: 0.19 N m |
| Joint damping | 0.0215855 N m s/rad | stall over no-load speed: 0.0216 |
| Top speed that follows (limit / damping) | 8.7 rad/s, 500 deg/s | no-load at 5.0 V: 500 deg/s |
| Position gain `kp` | 12.95 N m/rad: full torque at 0.83 degrees of error | not in the datasheet |
| Armature, dry friction | 0.0015 kg m^2, 0.01 N m | not in the datasheet |

## The numbers to train on (decided 2026-11-24)

The owner's decision: train on the reported numbers, give or take 4 to 5 % (first 3 %, widened the same day
because the one servo measured was 4 % under the datasheet's speed). In trainnr that is `actuator: model` (the
table above) with `dr_span: 0.05`. As of that day a span is recorded and has no effect: trainnr varies only a
fitted servo model, not a model's own actuators
([Trainnr-AI/trainnr#150](https://github.com/Trainnr-AI/trainnr/issues/150)), so every simulated servo is
exactly the datasheet's. The run `nano-walk-03-fast` was started with 0.03 set; since neither value acts, it was
left running, and the task takes 0.05 from the next run on.

## What one servo did on the bench

One new SCS0009 (ID 15), a bare horn, four AA cells at 5.7 to 5.8 V, through the firmware
([recordings and method](../../scripts/servo-bench/README.md), 2026-11-23 and 24).

| | Measured | Datasheet, scaled to 5.8 V |
|---|---|---|
| Top speed (the servo's own report, 60 degree jumps) | 557 deg/s | 580 deg/s |
| A 60 degree jump, 90 % done | 162 ms after the command was sent | 100 ms of travel at no-load speed, before any delay |
| Rest position after a jump | within 0.5 degrees (under two steps) | backlash 0.5 degrees or less |

- **The speed is the datasheet's**, 4 % under it. So the damping the model uses is about right.
- **It rests further from a target it was led to slowly than from one it jumped to.** After jumps it came to rest
  within 0.5 degrees. Led to a target at 150 deg/s (the pet run of 2026-11-24, before a hand was on it) it
  rested 0.7, 1.0 and 1.2 degrees away on three targets, on either side, and stayed there for the second and a
  half until the next one; on a fourth 2.2 degrees, when a hand may already have been on the horn. With an error
  that small its drive does not overcome its own friction. The model's stiff `kp` and small friction would rest
  0.04 degrees away. A walking policy trained on the model will count on small corrections that the real servo
  does not make.
- **The supply decides what it can do.** Fighting a hand, it pulled four AA cells from 5.7 V to under 4.3 V at
  once (the firmware switched it off both times). At a stall current of 1 A that is an internal resistance of
  1.4 ohm or more for the pack: these cells are no supply for it.
- **Not measured:** torque, in any form. The stall torque above is Feetech's claim. Either a pendulum of known
  mass on the horn or the horn pressed on a kitchen scale would check it.
